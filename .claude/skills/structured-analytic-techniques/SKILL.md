# Structured Analytic Techniques

## Purpose

Review whether an analytic product selected, executed, and sequenced the structured
technique that its stage, uncertainty, and stakes actually called for — a
perspective-breaking technique such as Red Team or Alternative Futures where
mirror-imaging or a single-outcome forecast is the risk, brainstorming and Outside-In
Thinking at conceptualization, Indicators to track a judgment afterward — rather than
reaching for whichever technique is already habitual regardless of the project's stage.
When no technique has been picked yet, this skill instead recommends and sequences one
for the problem at hand. Either way it audits or advises on the technique's fit and
execution, never the substantive conclusion it produces.

## When to use

- A judgment rests on a single hypothesis or a confident single-outcome forecast, and it
  is unclear whether a perspective-breaking technique was ever run to test whether a
  different framing survives.
- An assessment of a foreign leader, group, or organization assumes they reason the way
  the analyst would, rather than having been tested by a team steeped in that actor's own
  environment.
- Small anomalies are being explained away one at a time instead of tested against a
  deliberately surprising alternative account built by working backward from it.
- The caller is choosing or sequencing which structured technique fits a new or ongoing
  project — what to run at the start, throughout, during hypothesis testing, and before
  the judgment is finalized.
- A project moved straight from conceptualization to the factors already on the table,
  without first surveying the outside social, technological, economic, or political
  forces that could reshape the problem.
- A tradeoff decision is being argued qualitatively instead of structured first, with its
  attributes weighed through a formal method once real stakes are involved.
- A situation is being tracked over time, or a rival-hypothesis debate has become
  personalized, with no agreed list of observable indicators to depersonalize it and flag
  change.
- The assigned question reads as ill-posed or constraining, and it is unclear whether
  that framing was challenged before being taken as given.
- A topic has been owned by the same analyst or team for a long time, or a brainstorming
  session produced a flat or narrow set of hypotheses and the working conditions
  themselves may explain why — either calls for a deliberately fresh input such as a
  newcomer's view, lightweight political gaming, or a properly run two-phase session.
- The number of hypotheses being carried was fixed by convention rather than scaled to
  the uncertainty and stakes involved.

## Procedure

1. Determine the mode: if the caller submits a finished or drafted analytic judgment or
   forecast for critique, take the review path in steps 2-9; if the caller is instead
   choosing, designing, or sequencing a technique before any judgment exists, skip
   straight to the advise/compare path in step 10 rather than forcing a finding frame
   onto a decision that has not been made yet.
2. Check whether the product treats a single hypothesis or single-outcome forecast as
   settled without ever contesting it: was a Red Team run — an adversary-steeped team
   reasoning and writing in the first person, without caveats — or an Alternative Futures
   analysis built by crossing the two most critical, uncertain drivers into four future
   worlds (P004, P006)?
3. Where the judgment concerns a foreign leader, group, or organization, confirm it was
   tested against a team steeped in that actor's own culture and organization rather than
   assumed to mirror the analyst's own reasoning, and note that even a well-run Red Team
   only reduces, never eliminates, that mind-set (P004).
4. Where small anomalies are accumulating against the prevailing account, or a
   low-probability, high-consequence outcome is under-explored, check whether a
   perspective technique such as thinking backwards — assume the surprising outcome
   happened, then explain how — was used to shift the question from whether to how
   (P007).
5. Confirm the project's technique sequence matched its stage: brainstorming, a Key
   Assumptions Check, and Outside-In Thinking at the start; Indicators and ACH revisited
   throughout as evidence arrives; a contrarian or Red Team pass plus an
   intelligence-gap review during hypothesis testing; and, before finalizing, a re-check
   of key assumptions, a further brainstorm for missed hypotheses, Devil's Advocacy
   against an unquestioned consensus, and a key-indicators list (P011). Where
   brainstorming was the tool, confirm it ran as a two-phase divergent-then-convergent
   session with no idea censored, killer phrases banned, at least one outsider present,
   and rank left at the door (P026).
6. If Outside-In Thinking was due at conceptualization, confirm it started from broad,
   uncontrollable social, technological, economic, environmental, and political forces
   before narrowing to controllable factors, testing each candidate force against
   evidence rather than assuming its relevance (P039).
7. Confirm a tracking or rival-hypothesis judgment carries an Indicators or Signposts
   list of observable events — a separate expected-observables list per hypothesis when
   rivals are in play — and that a sharply divided debate is being depersonalized
   through an agreed list rather than personal argument (P033).
8. Check whether the assigned question was accepted as posed even though it reads as
   ill-posed or constraining, when the better move was pushing back up the chain with a
   reformulated question (P042); and whether a tradeoff decision was structured as a
   list, table, tree, or matrix before being argued qualitatively, with multiattribute
   utility and a sensitivity analysis run once real tradeoffs are at stake (P043).
9. Where the hypothesis count looks fixed by habit, or a topic has sat with the same
   analyst or team for a long time, confirm the count was scaled to the level of
   uncertainty and policy impact rather than convention, grouping like hypotheses once
   the set runs past about seven; check whether a fresh perspective — a newcomer or a
   lightweight political-gaming exercise — was at least considered; and whether the
   working conditions themselves (autonomy, professional security, a hands-off superior,
   small size) were favorable enough for creative technique use to succeed at all (P079,
   P080, P072, P030).
10. In advise or compare mode, match the technique to the driver instead — Red Team or
    Alternative Futures for mirror-imaging and single-outcome risk (P004, P006), a
    perspective technique for a surprising low-probability event (P007), Brainstorming
    and Outside-In Thinking at conceptualization (P026, P039), Indicators for ongoing
    tracking (P033), problem-structuring and multiattribute utility for a tradeoff
    (P043), political gaming or a deliberately fresh outside view when a topic has gone
    stale (P072, P079) — sequence multiple techniques per the project-stage order in
    P011, name each technique's trade-off when comparing two, and flag when the
    environment's own conditions (autonomy, security, size, supervision) will determine
    whether the chosen technique can actually produce anything (P030).
11. Emit the review's findings, or the advise/compare recommendation, highest-impact
    first, in the output contract format below.

## Inputs

- The analytic judgment, forecast, or draft product under review — or, in advise/compare
  mode, the problem statement, its uncertainty and stakes, and where the project sits in
  its timeline (start, ongoing, hypothesis testing, or pre-publication).
- Which hypotheses have been considered, and whether a rival account or forecast has been
  tested against the favored one.
- A record of which structured techniques, if any, have already been run, and at what
  point in the project.
- For a judgment about a foreign leader, group, or organization, what is known about that
  actor's own culture, organization, and history (needed to staff or evaluate a Red
  Team).
- For a tracking judgment, the Indicators or Signposts list currently in use, if any, and
  whether rival hypotheses each have their own list.
- For a tradeoff decision, the attributes being weighed and any existing structure (list,
  table, tree, or matrix).
- Whether the topic has been owned by the same analyst or team for an extended period,
  and what is known about the team's working conditions (autonomy, security,
  supervision, project size).

## Output

In review mode, per finding: name the reasoning gap (a stage-inappropriate or missing
technique, an untested single-outcome forecast, an unexamined foreign mind-set, a
personalized rival-hypothesis debate, an unstructured tradeoff, a stale hypothesis count
or topic), cite the principle(s) it rests on, name the corrective technique and how to
run it, state the residual uncertainty the correction leaves, and end with a concrete
next step. Order findings highest-impact first. Never issue the substantive analytic judgment or
forecast yourself — a finding names the reasoning gap and the corrective technique, not the answer.

In advise mode, name the recommended technique, cite the principle(s) behind it, and
state the residual uncertainty even once it is run. In compare mode, name each candidate
technique's trade-off and when it is stronger, tied to the same principles. Never close
any of the three with a bare technique name or checklist tick in place of this
structure.

## Anti-patterns to flag

- A single-outcome forecast or unchallenged hypothesis published with no Red Team or
  Alternative Futures pass ever attempted, despite real competing views or high
  uncertainty (P004, P006).
- A judgment about a foreign actor argued from what the analyst would do in that
  situation, with no team steeped in the actor's own culture and organization ever
  staffed to test it (P004).
- Anomalies accumulating against a prevailing account explained away one at a time, with
  no perspective technique — assuming the surprising event happened and working backward
  from it — ever used to test the alternative directly (P007).
- A technique run at the wrong project stage: brainstorming or a Key Assumptions Check
  skipped at the start, Indicators or ACH abandoned once set instead of revisited as
  evidence arrives, or no Devil's Advocacy run against an unquestioned consensus before
  publication (P011).
- A brainstorming session run with ideas censored on the spot, killer phrases allowed, no
  outsider present, or rank pulled to set an official line (P026).
- A stalled or unproductive brainstorming or technique-selection effort blamed on the
  analysts' talent, when the likelier cause is unfavorable conditions — no autonomy, no
  professional security, a hands-on superior, or an oversized project (P030).
- A situation tracked with no Indicators or Signposts list, or a rival-hypothesis debate
  argued personally with no agreed, depersonalizing list of observable events (P033).
- Analysis narrowed straight to the familiar, controllable factors with no Outside-In
  pass across the uncontrollable social, technological, economic, environmental, or
  political forces first (P039).
- An ill-posed or constraining question answered as given, with no push back up the
  chain for a better formulation (P042).
- A tradeoff argued qualitatively with no problem structure chosen first, or a real
  tradeoff never run through multiattribute utility and a sensitivity analysis (P043).
- A stale topic worked by the same analyst or team indefinitely with no lightweight
  political-gaming exercise or outside newcomer's view ever brought in (P072, P079).
- A hypothesis count fixed at some conventional number regardless of the uncertainty and
  policy stakes involved, with no grouping applied once the set grows past about seven
  (P080).
