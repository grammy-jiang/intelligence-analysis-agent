# Assessing Enemy Trust and Belief

## Purpose

Review how far the adversary is judged to trust a channel — the belief on which every deception rests. Trust is not assumed; it is read continuously from the enemy's own behaviour: the questions he asks, the payments he makes, the resources he invests, and how his handler behaves. This skill checks that a claim of the enemy's belief is evidenced, and that an agent is graded by the hard facts he can supply rather than his social access.

## When this applies

- the enemy service is trusted by its own high command

## Procedure

1. Confirm the enemy's trust in each agent is continuously assessed from the questions asked, the payments made, the sensitivity and reuse of the codes entrusted to him, the training and resources invested, remarks in personal contact, and secret intelligence — because standing oscillates and must be known before a critical task (P013).
2. Read the handler's investment: an enemy handler who manages his own superiors to reward and protect the agent, even securing him a decoration, is the strongest guarantor of the case and the surest proof of belief (P004).
3. Grade the agent by verifiable facts, not access — a well-connected agent relaying only gossip is low-grade, while a lowly seaman or wireless operator who observes hard facts is often the most valuable, because the enemy staff needs facts for its appreciations (P022).
4. Expect a well-established, trusted agent to be extremely hard to blow — when a deception is exposed the enemy prefers benign explanations (the agent was misled, the plan abandoned) over the truth (P021).
5. Choreograph recovery efforts to escalate gradually and appear not frightened while making it plain you are, so the enemy infers the material is genuinely important without suspecting a plant (P038).
6. Weight the source correctly: a veteran long-established agent carries far more enemy confidence than a new one, so use newly acquired agents only for short-term tactical work; and value rear or home-base sources over forward tactical ones (P064, P091).

## Principles to apply

Each rule below is a promoted principle of this package; cite its ID in a finding.

- **P004** (high) — Recognise that an enemy handler can become so invested in your agent's welfare that he manages his own superiors to reward and protect the agent, even securing him an enemy decoration; this deep investment is the strongest guarantor of the case and the surest proof of the enemy's belief.
- **P013** (high) — Continuously assess how far the enemy trusts each agent, using the questions asked, the payments made, the sensitivity and reuse of the codes and methods entrusted to him, the training and resources invested in him, remarks in personal contact, and secret intelligence, because an agent's standing oscillates and you must know it before relying on him for a critical task.
- **P021** (high) — Expect that a well-established, trusted agent is extremely hard to blow, because when a deception is exposed the enemy prefers benign explanations, that the agent was misled, that the plan was abandoned, or that the cover fooled him, over the truth, and established trust resists even blatant contrary evidence.
- **P022** (high) — Judge an agent's grade by the verifiable facts he can supply, not by his social access: a well-connected agent relaying only gossip or embassy rumour is low-grade, while a lowly placed seaman or wireless operator who observes hard facts is often the most valuable, because the enemy staff needs facts on which to base its appreciations.
- **P038** (high) — Choreograph your recovery efforts to escalate gradually and appear not frightened while making it plain you are, so the enemy infers the material is genuinely important without suspecting a plant.
- **P064** (high) — Recognise that a veteran, long-established agent carries far more enemy confidence than a new one, so use newly acquired agents only for short-term tactical work, not long-term strategic build-up.
- **P091** (medium) — Value information from sources further back from the front, in the rear or home base, as more reliable and valuable than forward tactical sources.

## Anti-patterns to flag

- Asserting the enemy trusts a channel without evidence from his questions, payments, and investment.
- Grading an agent by his social access rather than the verifiable facts he supplies.
- Using a newly acquired agent for long-term strategic deception that needs veteran credibility.

## Output

One finding per problem surfaced above, highest-risk first, each naming the tradecraft flaw and the principle it violates, the correction, the residual uncertainty (including who could still be deceiving whom), and a concrete next step. Never issue the caller's go/no-go: the corrected judgment and the residual risk are handed back to the operation's owner.
