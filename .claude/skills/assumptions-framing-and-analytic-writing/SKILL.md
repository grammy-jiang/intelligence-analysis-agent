# Assumptions Framing And Analytic Writing

## Purpose

Review whether an analytic product's assumptions are made explicit and open to challenge —
rather than left buried or treated as eliminable — whether the question behind the task was
framed correctly before the analysis started, and whether the write-up itself separates what is
known as fact from what is believed as opinion, delineates its chain of inference, and states the
degree and source of its remaining uncertainty. This skill also checks that a position already
committed to writing has not hardened against new evidence simply because it is already in print.
It audits the product's foundations, framing, and prose — never the substantive conclusion those
foundations support.

## When to use

- An analytic judgment or forecast is under review, and it is unclear whether its assumptions were
  made explicit and open to challenge — or simply carried over unstated, on the mistaken premise
  that objectivity requires eliminating assumptions rather than exposing them (P047).
- An argument's chain of inference is being scrutinized, and its linchpin assumption — the one
  premise the conclusion cannot survive without — has not been named (P073).
- A judgment reads confidently throughout, and it is unclear where the write-up draws the line
  between what is established as fact or reliably reported and what the analyst believes without
  evidence cited to back it (P057).
- A finished analytic product states a conclusion, and it is unclear whether the write-up specifies
  the degree and source of its uncertainty and lays out its chain of inference, or simply asserts
  the result (P074).
- New evidence has arrived after an assessment was already committed to writing, and the review
  needs to check whether the case is still being treated as open, rather than defended because the
  call is already in print (P059).
- An analytic task is being scoped, or its scoping is under review, and it needs checking whether
  the right question is being asked and whether any tradeoff between analytic quality and the
  deadline has been raised with whoever set the task, rather than absorbed silently (P075).

## Procedure

1. Identify the assumption(s) the judgment's chain of inference actually depends on — the linchpin
   assumption — and check whether the write-up names it explicitly rather than leaving it buried
   inside phrasing that reads as established fact (P073).
2. Test each surfaced assumption for challengeability: can a reader see it stated, see what would
   have to be true for it to be wrong, and see it treated as one candidate among others rather than
   as a non-issue removed by fiat? Objectivity here means making assumptions explicit and open to
   challenge, not eliminating them or substituting exhaustive, assumption-free data collection —
   which was found less accurate than forming and testing an explicit hypothesis even in medical
   diagnosis (P047).
3. Walk the write-up and sort every stated or implied conclusion into "known as fact or reliably
   reported" versus "believed as opinion"; confirm every opinion-class sentence is backed by
   evidence sufficient to meet a show-me-your-evidence standard (P057).
4. Check that the product's stated confidence names the degree and the source of its uncertainty,
   rather than resting on an unsupported adjective such as "likely" or "assessed with confidence,"
   and that the chain from evidence to conclusion is laid out rather than merely asserted (P074).
5. If the assessment under review was already committed to writing before new evidence arrived,
   check whether that evidence changed the position or whether premature closure — and the vested
   interest both analyst and organization acquire once a call is in print — kept the original
   judgment frozen regardless of merit (P059).
6. If the task's scope is itself in question, check whether the analysis answers the question that
   was actually needed, whether a better formulation was raised back up the chain of command where
   warranted, and whether any tradeoff between analytic quality and the deadline was flagged to
   whoever set the task rather than resolved unilaterally (P075).
7. Emit findings highest-risk-to-the-judgment first, each in the name-the-flaw / correction /
   residual-uncertainty / next-step format, and stop at the reasoning and the writing — never issue
   the substantive judgment, forecast, or estimate the product itself is making.

## Inputs

- The analytic judgment, forecast, or product under review, including its full write-up — not just
  the headline conclusion.
- The assumption(s) the analysis rests on, whether already stated in the product or only
  discoverable by tracing its reasoning.
- Which claims in the write-up are treated as fact or reliably reported information and which are
  opinion, and the evidence cited for each opinion-class claim.
- Whether the assessment has previously been committed to writing or published, and any new
  evidence that has arrived since.
- The question or task as originally posed, and any deadline or resource constraint placed on the
  analysis.

## Output

Per finding: name the flaw (an implicit or unchallengeable assumption, an opinion written as fact,
an unstated degree or source of uncertainty, a scoping mismatch, or a position frozen by premature
closure), apply the correction (name the assumption and make it testable, cite the evidence a
claimed fact or opinion actually rests on, state the uncertainty's degree and source, reframe the
question or flag the quality/deadline tradeoff, or reopen the case for the new evidence), state the
residual uncertainty the correction leaves, and end with a concrete next step. Order findings
highest-risk-to-the-judgment first. Never close a review by issuing the substantive judgment in
place of this structure.

## Anti-patterns to flag

- An assumption doing real work in the argument that is never named, leaving a reader no way to
  see — let alone challenge — the premise the conclusion actually rests on (P073, P047).
- A judgment that treats "objective" as meaning "free of assumptions," or as best achieved through
  exhaustive, assumption-free data collection, rather than through explicit, challengeable
  hypotheses — a preference the source found more accurate even in medical diagnosis (P047).
- Opinion written in the same register as fact, with no evidence cited and no sense that the claim
  could be challenged on a show-me-your-evidence standard (P057).
- A stated confidence with no degree or source behind it — a bare "likely" or "assessed with high
  confidence" standing in for an actual chain of inference (P074).
- An assessment that keeps its original call after new evidence arrives, not because the evidence
  was weighed and found wanting, but because the call is already in print and both the analyst and
  the organization now have a vested interest in it (P059).
- A task answered exactly as originally posed with no check on whether it was the right question,
  and no record that a quality-versus-deadline tradeoff was ever surfaced to whoever set the task
  (P075).
