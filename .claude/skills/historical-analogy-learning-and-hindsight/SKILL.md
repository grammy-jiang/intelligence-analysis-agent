# Historical Analogy Learning And Hindsight

## Purpose

Review how an analytic judgment chose its historical analogy, drew a lesson from a past
success or failure, and wrote up an outcome once it was already known. Check that the analogy
was chosen for a genuinely relevant feature rather than an irrelevant one, that the claimed
lesson has been tested for spuriousness and self-selection rather than merely asserted, that a
success or failure is not over-credited to the policy itself while luck, cost, and foregone
alternatives go unweighed, and that the write-up preserves the surprise the outcome once
produced rather than reading the past as though it had always been predictable. This skill
audits the analogy, the lesson, and the after-the-fact account — not the substantive judgment
the current case calls for.

## When to use

- A historical analogy is anchoring the interpretation of a current situation, or the lessons
  of the most recent war or crisis are being applied as the template for a new one.
- A state's leadership is reading a present situation through the lens of the revolution,
  founding struggle, or formative career episode that shaped it.
- A judgment leans on a single vivid case, or a short first-hand visit, rather than a wider
  historical reference class.
- A lesson is being drawn from whether a past policy or decision succeeded or failed, before
  luck, cost, and the alternatives foregone have been ruled out.
- A claim that a past experience shaped a person's later view, or that a generation's formative
  years explain its outlook, has not been tested for spuriousness or self-selection.
- A notably successful policy is being repeated, or an expert's confident intuition is being
  trusted, without checking that the new case truly resembles the old one or that the feedback
  behind the intuition was accurate.
- A write-up produced after the outcome is known reads that outcome as though it had always
  been predictable, or a developing situation is being read as steadily confirming a single
  conclusion over time.

## Procedure

1. Establish the object under review: the analogy, precedent, or historical lesson being
   invoked, the present case it is applied to, and whether the eventual outcome is already
   known. Fix this before judging whether the analogy fits or the lesson holds.
2. Test why the governing analogy was chosen: reject a frame selected for a rationally
   irrelevant feature, such as whether one's own nation took part, in favor of one chosen by
   studying the actual causes of the earlier outcome, and watch for the two specific
   distortions that inflate a rival's apparent hostility — reading it as a centralized planner,
   and crediting its friendly acts to oneself while blaming its unfriendly acts on unprovoked
   hostility (P012). Confirm the frame itself was chosen deliberately, since the frame applied
   to identical evidence drives the conclusion and an institution's advance frame can delay
   recognizing a developing situation (P124).
3. If the most recent war or confrontation supplies the template, check that the new case
   truly resembles it rather than being just another familiar instance whose recurrence
   probability should not shift sharply — a shift that is largest, and least accurate, for
   amateurs and short-history nations with few competing analogies (P043). If a leadership's
   formative revolution or independence struggle supplies the frame, check whether it is
   projecting its own success-recipe, or its domestic-opponent image, onto an unrelated
   foreign situation (P037).
4. Apply the tests for a genuine historical lesson before crediting it: it is more likely real
   when people of differing personalities and beliefs learn the same lesson, when an
   experience changes a mind, or when an early response recurs in a situation others see as
   very different — and more likely spurious when the earlier and later situations are too
   similar or the person chose to enter the formative one (P002). Apply the same
   self-selection logic to a claim that a past event shaped an actor's predispositions
   generally (P055), and to a generational or early-experience claim, crediting the influence
   only when it is driven by situations faced rather than strategies chosen and is shared
   across people of differing characteristics (P038). Before crediting history with shaping a
   present view at all, rule out that a strikingly incorrect reading of the past was actually
   produced by current preferences (P028).
5. Check the record the lesson was actually drawn from: confirm the context needed to
   interpret a past act reached the part of the government that had to interpret it, given the
   uneven internal distribution of classified information (P137), and check whether an agent's
   unofficial remarks contradicting the official message were accounted for as a possible
   deliberate signal or sign of internal trends, rather than left out of the reconstructed
   picture entirely (P039). A lesson drawn from an incomplete record is unreliable regardless
   of how sound the later reasoning looks.
6. Test for outcome-driven distortion: check whether a success is over-credited to the policy
   while its costs, better alternatives, and luck go unweighed, or a failure is blamed on
   rejected alternatives never actually tried, without allowing that the policy may have been
   the best available given the information at the time — and watch for an anomalous result
   explained away by special factors to preserve a prior belief (P003). Check whether an
   earlier success is credited as the cause of a later, repeated policy when the repetition
   may instead reflect an unreexamined decision process reapplied, or shared underlying
   variables driving both choices (P172). Check whether a notably successful policy is being
   over-applied to a new case assumed similar, without asking why it worked or whether the
   actor's own success has already changed the environment that made it work (P099).
7. Weigh vividness and firsthand exposure: give little weight to a single vivid anecdote or
   case history unless it is known to be typical, and distrust a short firsthand visit that
   gives undue weight to personally witnessed impressions over the larger, more diagnostic
   body of evidence (P001, P062). Check whether a firsthand experience is producing a
   disproportionate predisposition merely because participating and witnessing teach more
   than reading, not because the case was representative — the specific position a person
   held within the event also leaves sensitivities others lack (P098).
8. Before crediting an expert's confident intuitive judgment, check that the environment
   supplied repeated exposure to similar cases with accurate feedback; conditions such as
   foreign policy usually fail this test because the important cases are few and feedback is
   weak — but remember that intuition can still be right, and a heavily deliberated decision
   that turned out badly may reflect a hard problem rather than a bad process (P011).
9. Check the residual perceptual factors that color a historical judgment: confirm a balanced
   image of an actor is a rational read of a genuinely high prior rather than a mask over a
   real inconsistency (P061); check whether training has shifted the reviewed party's
   perceptual threshold so that it is prone to seeing its trained-for phenomenon even when
   absent (P058); and, for a situation tracked through incremental reports, confirm each new
   report was tested on its own merits rather than assumed to confirm the same conclusion as
   time passed, since premature closure — not genuine clarification — usually produces the
   apparent convergence (P030).
10. For any judgment written after the outcome is known, apply the counterfactual test: would
    the analyst have been surprised had the opposite occurred, would the consumer have
    believed the opposite report, would the overseer have found the opposite outcome
    predictable from the information actually available at the time (P015)? Confirm the
    write-up preserves the surprise the outcome once produced rather than defaulting to a
    "knew it all along" reading that forecloses exactly the evidence of where a belief failed
    (P195).
11. Emit findings highest-impact first, each in the name-the-mechanism / correction /
    residual-uncertainty / next-step format.

## Inputs

- The analytic judgment, forecast, or product under review, including any historical analogy,
  precedent, or past-experience-based lesson it invokes.
- The present case or situation the analogy or lesson is being applied to, so its actual
  resemblance to the precedent can be tested.
- What is known about why the earlier case, policy, or interaction actually occurred or
  succeeded or failed — including internal, classified, or agent-reported context beyond the
  version the judgment relies on, where available.
- Whether the outcome under review is already known, so the review can reconstruct the
  uncertainty that existed before it was known.
- Any track record showing how this analyst, agency, or actor has drawn on history, intuition,
  or a formative experience before.

## Output

Per finding: name the historical-learning or hindsight flaw (an irrelevantly chosen analogy, an
untested lesson, outcome-driven over-crediting, vivid-case or firsthand overweighting, an
ungrounded intuition claim, an incomplete record, or hindsight's erased surprise), apply the
correction (widen the reference class and test the causes of the earlier outcome, run the
spuriousness or self-selection tests, weigh costs and foregone alternatives against the bare
outcome, discount the anecdote against the more diagnostic evidence, or reconstruct the
pre-outcome uncertainty with the analyst/consumer/overseer counterfactual), state the residual
uncertainty the correction leaves, and end with a concrete next step. Order findings
highest-impact first. Never let a corrected write-up still read as though the past had always
been predictable.

## Anti-patterns to flag

- An analogy or precedent chosen because it is vivid or emotionally salient — such as whether
  one's own nation took part — rather than because its actual causes match the present case,
  including reflexively reading the other side as a centralized planner and crediting its
  friendly acts to oneself while blaming its unfriendly acts on unprovoked hostility (P012,
  P124).
- The most recent war or crisis treated as the template for a new one, or a leadership's
  formative revolution or independence struggle projected onto an unrelated adversary, without
  testing whether the new case actually resembles the old one (P043, P037).
- A lesson credited as genuine without the spuriousness tests — accepted even though the
  earlier and later situations are nearly identical, the person chose to enter the formative
  situation, or the same underlying disposition may have produced both the experience and the
  later view (P002, P028, P055, P038).
- A success story over-credited to the policy that produced it, with its costs and the
  alternatives foregone left unweighed, while a failure is blamed entirely on rejected
  alternatives never actually tried, or a run of triumphs assumed repeatable without asking
  whether the actor's own success already changed the environment (P003, P099, P172).
- A single vivid anecdote, case history, or short firsthand visit outweighing the larger, more
  diagnostic body of evidence, or a firsthand experience treated as representative rather than
  as the lucky or unlucky draw the position happened to produce (P001, P062, P098).
- An expert's confident intuition accepted on authority alone, without asking whether the
  environment that produced it supplied the regular, accurate feedback intuition actually
  needs (P011).
- Advice from a trained specialist accepted without asking whether that same training also
  makes them prone to seeing their trained-for danger where it does not exist (P058).
- A developing situation's incremental reports all read as confirming the same conclusion,
  mistaking premature closure for a picture that has genuinely come into focus (P030).
- A balanced or coherent image of an actor accepted as read, with no check for whether it is a
  genuinely rational read of a high base rate or is instead masking a real inconsistency
  (P061).
- An agent's unofficial remarks, or context locked behind classified compartmentalization, left
  out of the reconstructed record, so the lesson is drawn from an incomplete picture of what
  actually happened (P039, P137).
- A write-up produced after the outcome is known that reads the result as though it had always
  been obvious, with no reconstruction of the surprise or uncertainty that existed
  beforehand — the "knew it all along" reflex (P015, P195).
