# Cognitive Biases And Dual Process Reasoning

## Purpose

Audits an analytic judgment or forecast for the fast, intuitive (System 1) failure modes
that corrupt reasoning before slower, structured correction has a chance to run — a
preferred conclusion defended by discrediting dissonant evidence while a confirming
result sails through unquestioned, an emotional stance quietly deciding which argument
feels compelling, and a validated formula overridden on ordinary case knowledge instead
of a rare individuating fact. It also checks whether the judgment was built by actively
searching for missing evidence and by mixing theory with data, rather than letting one
vivid case harden into a general rule, and whether any reported performance is honest
about the gap between raw, unadjusted accuracy and whatever was adjusted after the fact.
This skill audits the reasoning's psychological honesty — including whether the reviewed
party keeps open the standing possibility that they are mistaken — not the substance of
the conclusion itself.

## When to use

- A judgment's written case needs auditing for whether disconfirming evidence received the
  same scrutiny as confirming evidence, or whether an inconvenient finding was quietly
  neutralized by questioning its source, method, or motives rather than its content.
- The case for a conclusion reads as heavily one-directional, and the actual balance of
  supporting-versus-opposing considerations needs to be counted rather than assumed.
- A prediction or estimate rests on information that was available but never actively
  sought, and it is unclear whether that gap was noticed before the judgment was finalized.
- The reviewed party holds a visibly strong like or dislike toward the subject, and the
  argument they find most persuasive lines up suspiciously well with what they would
  prefer to be true.
- An analyst or decision-maker wants to set aside a validated predictive formula for their
  own case read, and it needs checking whether they hold a rare, individuating fact or just
  ordinary case knowledge the formula already accounts for.
- Deciding whether a one-case lesson actually generalizes, or whether the reasoning leans
  entirely on preconception or entirely on raw data with nothing to check either against
  the other.
- A performance claim mixes raw, unadjusted results with subjectively adjusted ones, and it
  is unclear how much of the reported accuracy is genuine ex ante skill versus
  after-the-fact credit.
- A conclusion is delivered with total conviction and no acknowledgment, however small,
  that it could be wrong.

## Procedure

1. Check whether disconfirming evidence was interrogated as hard as confirming evidence.
   Watch for a double standard: the same evidence quality earns credibility when it fits
   the preferred conclusion and gets neutralized — by attacking motives, authenticity, or
   interpretation — roughly four times more often when it does not, with an assurance of
   rigor barely closing the gap (P009, P051).
2. Count, or estimate, the ratio of supporting to opposing considerations raised in the
   written case. The average expert favors the preferred outcome by roughly three to one;
   a near one-directional ratio signals lopsided thinking rather than a genuinely weighed
   judgment (P053).
3. Check whether a vital prediction or estimate rests on information that was available and
   significant but never actively sought. Treat that search failure as a reasoning flaw in
   its own right, not a neutral gap, since sound judgment requires going to look for
   evidence rather than only weighing what happened to arrive (P038).
4. Check whether an emotional stance toward the subject is doing the arguing. Under the
   affect heuristic, likes and dislikes decide which arguments feel compelling, and this
   pull dominates most strongly exactly where the emotional stake is highest (P064).
5. If a validated formula was overridden by human judgment, examine the stated reason. A
   genuine broken-leg exception is a rare, decisive, individuating fact outside the
   formula's inputs — not ordinary case knowledge dressed up as special insight, which is
   what people wrongly believe justifies an override (P065).
6. Check whether the reasoning mixes theory-driven and data-driven thinking rather than
   leaning on only one, and whether a single case has been allowed to harden into a stable
   lesson or philosophy of history. A trait or lesson that reverses depending on whose
   interests are served should not be generalized from one instance (P066).
7. Where performance or accuracy is reported, confirm the raw, unadjusted score is stated
   alongside any requested adjustment, and treat a wide gap between the two with suspicion
   — unadjusted ex ante accuracy is a different claim from an ex post adjustment that only
   measures how well the gap was closed afterward (P067).
8. Check that the judgment under review — and this review itself — keeps open the
   declaratory stance that the conclusion could be mistaken, a standing check on
   conviction (P082).
9. Emit findings highest-risk-to-the-judgment first, each in the name-the-flaw /
   correction / residual-uncertainty / next-step format.

## Inputs

- The analytic judgment or forecast under review, together with the written case made for
  it.
- The evidence treated as confirming the conclusion and the evidence treated as
  disconfirming it, so the two can be compared for equal scrutiny.
- Any information that was available and significant to the question but not actively
  sought.
- Signals of the reviewed party's emotional stance toward the subject, where the affect
  heuristic may be in play.
- Where a validated predictive formula exists: its output, any human override, and the
  stated reason for the override.
- The historical case or precedent, if any, that the judgment generalizes from.
- Any reported performance record, with both raw and subjectively adjusted figures if both
  exist.
- The confidence or hedging language used in the final write-up.

## Output

Per finding: name the cognitive-bias or dual-process flaw (motivated skepticism,
asymmetric scrutiny, an unchecked affect heuristic, an unjustified formula override, an
over-generalized one-case lesson, a raw-versus-adjusted scoring gap, or a missing
declaratory hedge), apply the principle(s) by ID, name the corrective (equalize the
scrutiny applied to both sides, recount the pro-versus-con ratio, go and seek the missing
evidence, separate the emotional stance from the argument, restrict formula overrides to a
genuine broken-leg fact, report raw alongside adjusted scores, restate the declaratory
stance), state the residual uncertainty the correction leaves, and end with a concrete
next step. Order findings highest-risk-to-the-judgment first. Never substitute this
review's own substantive judgment, forecast, or estimate for the one under review.

## Anti-patterns to flag

- A confirming result waved through without question while a disconfirming result of
  equal quality is picked apart on method, motive, or authenticity — the same double
  standard dressed up as rigor (P009, P051).
- A written case that runs heavily one-directional, offering barely a
  counter-consideration, with no check on how lopsided the pro-versus-con ratio has
  actually become (P053).
- A prediction resting on information that was sitting available and significant, but
  nobody went and looked for it, waved through as a neutral gap rather than a reasoning
  failure (P038).
- An argument that tracks the reviewed party's likes and dislikes more closely than the
  evidence — the most emotionally comfortable conclusion is also the one argued most
  forcefully (P064).
- A validated formula set aside for "extra case knowledge" that is really just more of
  the same kind of information the formula already used, rather than a rare, decisive
  individuating fact (P065).
- A single case elevated into a stable philosophy of history or a general lesson, or a
  judgment built on preconception alone or raw data alone with nothing to check either
  against the other (P066).
- A performance claim that reports only the subjectively adjusted score, or shows a wide
  gap between raw and adjusted figures with no explanation, blurring genuine ex ante
  accuracy with ex post credit (P067).
- A conclusion delivered with total conviction and no room, even nominally, for the
  possibility that it is wrong (P082).
