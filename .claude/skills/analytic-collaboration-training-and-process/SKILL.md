# Analytic Collaboration Training And Process

## Purpose

Audit the training, team design, and institutional review process behind an analytic judgment
or forecast — not the judgment's content, but whether the analyst and the shop around them were
set up to reason well. This skill checks whether the reasoning process itself was trained and
coached rather than only the writing, whether competing-view procedures were institutionalized
rather than staged for show, whether management mandated a systematic method on the issues that
warranted it, and whether postmortems and measurement stayed rigorous and unbiased. It audits
the scaffolding around the judgment, never the judgment itself.

## When to use

- A judgment or forecast came out of a team, and it is unclear whether that team was actually
  psychologically safe, diverse in perspective, and organized around a shared purpose — or
  produced groupthink dressed up as consensus.
- A consensus formed after a designated devil's-advocate or similar role weighed in, and it is
  unclear whether the agreement is genuine convergence (people who previously held a different
  view coming around against their own position or interest) or an artifact of the role itself
  manufacturing false confidence that dissent was heard.
- A high-stakes or deception-prone issue was carried on a single-outcome analysis, and whether
  management should have mandated — or did mandate — a systematic process such as Analysis of
  Competing Hypotheses needs checking.
- A draft is moving through pre-publication review, and the review needs to confirm it actually
  interrogated the analyst's unstated assumptions, the alternatives considered and rejected, and
  what would change the analyst's mind — ideally by critical thinkers from outside the author's
  branch.
- An intelligence failure, a stale estimate, or a suspiciously well-confirmed conclusion needs
  diagnosing for whether negative evidence went unweighted or an organizational incentive or
  culture problem is at play, rather than defaulting to blaming one analyst's psychology.
- An analyst-development or training plan is under review, to check it targets the analyst's own
  reasoning process — coaching, postmortems on both failures and successes, retrospective
  evaluation, practice by doing — rather than only writing craft or one-off classroom instruction.
- The analytic process itself is being designed or audited: whether it institutionalizes
  competing-view procedures, periodic ground-up re-examination, honest expectation-setting with
  consumers about analysis's limits, and blind independent coding wherever results are measured.

## Procedure

1. Identify which process object is under review — a team's collaborative setup, a group's
   convergence on a judgment, a management decision about formalizing method, a
   training/development plan, an organizational failure being diagnosed, or a measurement/coding
   exercise — before applying the checks below.
2. Check the team or group behind the judgment for psychological safety, a shared "we" purpose,
   and genuine diversity of perspective rather than ability alone, since these — not raw
   individual skill — predict the aggregation gains and open-mindedness a good process should
   show (P015).
3. If a devil's-advocate or similar role was used, do not credit the resulting consensus at face
   value: check whether people who previously held a different view converged independently,
   especially against their own prior position or self-interest, and discount any convergence
   the role itself could have manufactured by letting the group feel falsely open-minded (P008).
4. For a high-stakes or deception-prone issue, check whether management rejected a
   single-outcome analysis and mandated a systematic process such as Analysis of Competing
   Hypotheses — requiring the alternatives considered, why each was judged less likely, and an
   explicit statement that events may not turn out as expected (P010).
5. Trace whether the analyst's or team's training and self-examination were directed inward at
   their own thinking and reasoning process — not only at organizational procedure, method, or
   the substantive topic — and whether that training was coached and learned by doing rather
   than delivered as classroom instruction alone (P001, P022, P071).
6. When diagnosing an intelligence failure, a stale confirmed estimate, or a lapse in
   evidence-weighing, check whether the organization's incentive structure or culture — not only
   one analyst's psychology — let negative evidence (the dogs that did not bark) go unweighted,
   and whether training and discipline were in place to counter that (P024).
7. Check that management supports periodically re-examining key problems from the ground up
   rather than relying on incremental adjustment alone, and that consumers were educated about
   analysis's limitations as well as its capabilities rather than sold false certainty (P040).
8. Confirm the analytic process itself institutionalizes procedures that surface competing
   views — analytic debate, devil's advocacy, competitive analysis, peer review, outside
   expertise — and rewards weighing multiple hypotheses over accepting the first credible one
   (P058).
9. For a draft under pre-publication review, confirm the review explicitly questioned the
   analyst's mental model — what unstated assumptions underlie the judgment, what alternatives
   were considered and why rejected, what would change the analyst's mind — and that reviewers
   included capable critical thinkers from outside the author's branch, not only same-branch
   colleagues who share the mind-set (P063).
10. If a measurement or scoring exercise is in scope, confirm coding was performed by independent
    coders blind to the hypotheses, so the analyst's own biases could not leak into the result
    (P081).
11. Emit findings highest-impact first, each naming the process flaw, the failure it enables, the
    corrective, and the residual uncertainty, and close with a concrete next step.

## Inputs

- The analytic judgment, forecast, or draft under review, and whether it was produced by an
  individual or a group.
- What is known about the team's setup: size, roles (including any devil's-advocate or similar
  role), psychological safety, and diversity of perspective.
- Any record of who held which view before and after a consensus formed, including whether
  convergence ran against a person's own prior position or self-interest.
- The issue's stakes and deception risk, and whether a systematic method such as Analysis of
  Competing Hypotheses was mandated, considered, or skipped.
- The analyst's or team's training and development history — what it targeted and how it was
  delivered — and any record of postmortems or retrospective evaluation.
- The organization's process design: which competing-view procedures are institutionalized, how
  consumers are briefed on analysis's limits, and whether periodic ground-up re-examination
  happens.
- The pre-publication review record — who reviewed the draft and what was asked — and, if
  scoring or coding is in scope, whether coders were independent and blind to the hypotheses.

## Output

Per finding: name the process flaw (a devil's-advocate role credited at face value instead of
weighed for genuine independent convergence, an unmandated single-outcome analysis on a
high-stakes or deception-prone issue, training aimed at writing instead of the reasoning process,
a pre-publication review that never questioned the analyst's mental model, non-blind or
non-independent coding, or a competing-view procedure never actually institutionalized), apply
the correction implied by the relevant principle (weigh independent convergence rather than the
role's face value, mandate a systematic method, redirect training and coaching inward and make it
practice-based, bring in outside critical thinkers, use blind independent coders, institutionalize
the missing procedure), state the residual uncertainty the correction leaves, and end with a
concrete next step. Order findings highest-impact first. Never substitute a bare pass/fail
verdict for this structure, and never produce the substantive analytic judgment yourself.

## Anti-patterns to flag

- A devil's-advocate or similar role credited as proof the group considered dissent, with the
  false confidence of having been open-minded substituted for evidence of genuine independent
  convergence (P008).
- A team or group lacking psychological safety or genuine diversity of perspective, credited with
  an aggregation gain its composition could not structurally have produced (P015).
- A high-stakes or deception-prone issue carried on a single-outcome analysis with no mandate for
  a systematic method like Analysis of Competing Hypotheses, no stated alternatives, and no
  acknowledgment that events may diverge from expectation (P010).
- Training and self-examination aimed only at writing quality, organizational procedure, or the
  substantive topic, never turned inward on the analyst's own thinking and reasoning (P001, P022).
- Analytic-thinking development delivered as one-off classroom instruction with no coaching and
  no practice-by-doing (P071).
- An intelligence failure or a suspiciously well-confirmed conclusion blamed solely on one
  analyst's psychology, with the organization's incentive structure, culture, or neglected
  negative evidence never examined (P024).
- Analysis run only incrementally, with key problems never re-examined from the ground up, and
  consumers never told what analysis cannot do (P040).
- A process design with no institutionalized competing-view procedure — no analytic debate,
  devil's advocacy, competitive analysis, peer review, or outside expertise sought — and the
  first credible hypothesis rewarded over weighing several (P058).
- Pre-publication review performed only by same-branch colleagues who share the analyst's
  mind-set, without asking what assumptions were unstated, what alternatives were rejected and
  why, or what would change the analyst's mind (P063).
- Coding or scoring performed by coders who know the hypotheses, or by the analyst themselves,
  letting bias leak into the measurement (P081).
