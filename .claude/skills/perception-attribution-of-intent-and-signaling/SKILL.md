# Perception Attribution Of Intent And Signaling

## Purpose

Review how an analytic product reads another actor's behavior, image, and signals, and check
whether the inference it draws about intent, disposition, or coordination is earned by the
evidence or is an artifact of a standing bias. Behavior is never self-explanatory: observers
habitually read a rival as more centralized and coordinated than it is, let a firsthand image fix
so firmly that they miss when the actor changes, credit their own influence to an asymmetry of
information they cannot see past, and react to the intent they impute rather than the harm or
help actually done. This skill audits that reading — how an image, signal, or attribution of
intent was formed and whether it holds up — not the substantive call about what the actor will
do next.

## When to use

- An analytic product reads a foreign government's, or an entire alliance's, disparate actions as
  a single coordinated plan, or treats weapons procurement as proof of a coherent strategy.
- A judgment attributes another actor's behavior to disposition, character, or a long-held value
  without first checking what the immediate situation alone would produce.
- A forecast or assessment rests on the belief that an adversary won't fight, or that a habitually
  aggressive actor is renewing aggression, and that belief itself needs testing against how much
  it is doing the perceptual work.
- The read of ambiguous evidence needs checking against what may be steering it independent of
  the actor's real disposition — a cultural label, a rehearsed contingency plan, or the emotional
  register of a crisis.
- An estimate treats an early-formed or firsthand image of an actor as still reliable, or
  dismisses the actor's current signals because "we already understand this actor."
- A message, warning, or signal is being assessed for what it will mean to its receiver, not for
  what the sender intended it to mean.
- A response to a harmful act, or credit given for a helpful one, is being calibrated and needs
  grading by the intent inferred rather than the act's raw size.
- Officials' stated reasons for a decision, or a claim that "there was no choice," are being taken
  as reliable evidence of the decision's true cause.
- A stream of individually weak or ambiguous reports needs to be read as a series rather than
  assessed one at a time.

## Procedure

1. Establish the object under review: the specific behavior, message, or event; the actor(s)
   involved; and the inference about intent, disposition, or coordination the product draws from
   it. Fix this before judging whether that inference is earned.
2. Check how the product assigns cause. Confirm it separates what the situation alone would
   produce from what reveals this actor's disposition — learning the most from a costly or
   unusual act and the least from a weak state's small concession to a stronger one (P109) — and
   that a claimed "there was no choice" is tested against whether a differently placed actor would
   have felt the same compulsion, not accepted merely because the decision-maker felt it (P157).
   Confirm perception is credited with causal work only where it demonstrably shaped the outcome,
   not by the comfortable shortcut of assuming any perception must explain the act because the
   actor held it, and not where a substitutable trigger event could explain the same outcome
   (P091). Confirm the act was reconstructed for the internal process behind it — whether it was
   controllable, understood, or coerced — since no act is self-explanatory and that reading
   changes both the prediction and the appropriate response (P127), and confirm the resulting
   claim is left probabilistic rather than stated as a settled law (P108).
3. Check whether the product takes an actor's own stated reasons, or his own forecast of his
   future behavior, at face value. Stated reasons are unreliable evidence of true motivation,
   since much cognition is unconscious and the reasons offered can be a post-hoc story rather than
   the actual cause (P024); an actor's own prediction of what he will do is no more reliable,
   since decision-makers often cannot foresee their own behavior when events outrun imagination or
   when they are avoiding a hard choice — predict what he will actually do instead (P130).
4. Check whether the product reads one government's disparate, unrelated moves as a single
   coordinated plan, or its weapons procurement as proof of a coherent strategy, when bureaucratic
   bargaining, accident, or unrelated inter-service conflict explains them at least as well (P013,
   P017, P081). Check separately whether the reading itself comes from a source especially prone
   to see plots: an operational code that treats nothing as accidental, a habit of long-range
   planning, low tolerance for ambiguity, or a conflict setting where the adversary's internal
   diversity is invisible all inflate this bias (P007). Weigh whether treating the actor as a
   unitary, centrally planned actor is nonetheless the most useful working model available — it
   can outperform an incomplete faction-level account when the contending interests are fairly
   stable, and when it is unclear which model fits a case, the method that works in the most cases
   beats probability-matching between them (P176). Extend the same check to a coalition: confirm
   the product is not crediting an alliance with more joint planning and unity than partners who
   cannot see each other's hand actually have (P175), and confirm an agreement resting on partners
   who hold different underlying images is flagged as fragile rather than settled, since such
   agreement tends to be short-lived and a dispute rooted in differing perceptions gets argued
   unproductively until that is recognized (P158).
5. Check whether a strong prior expectation, not the evidence, is doing the perceptual work:
   confirm that minimal or ambiguous information was not treated as sufficient to confirm what the
   analyst already expected, and watch for the self-fulfilling loop in which an analyst's
   expectation shapes field reporting, which then feeds the analyst's own certainty back (P005).
   Apply the same check to a paired belief that an adversary won't fight and would lose easily if
   it did: don't flag that pairing as automatically irrational, since expecting to win is itself
   often the cause of the accompanying low estimate that the enemy will act — while still
   remaining skeptical of any claim that one side holds a dominant strategy, which the world
   rarely justifies (P023). Confirm a reading of renewed aggression from a habitually aggressive
   actor is not simply perceptual readiness from past patterns overriding other, equally available
   explanations of the same ambiguous evidence (P164).
6. Check what is shaping the reading behind a reported perception, independent of the actor's
   actual disposition. Look for the evoked set — the immediate concerns and information dominating
   a person's thought at the time — which can make an ambiguous figure read as a number to someone
   already primed with numbers, or the same decisive gamble read as astute when it wins and
   impulsive when it loses (P096); for a cultural template, an explicit label, or a prior
   instruction, which shapes perception independent of familiarity so the same ambiguous stimulus
   reads differently once it has been named or framed a certain way (P110); for a rehearsed
   contingency plan, which primes decision-makers to see later events as resembling the planned
   scenario and calling for the very plan they prepared, even without new evidence making that
   scenario more likely (P170); and for the prevailing emotional register of a crisis, since anger
   favors action and fear favors restraint, so a mood shifting toward fear should read as making
   slower, cooperative options look more attractive even absent new evidence (P069).
7. Check how and when the product's working image of the actor formed. An image built on little
   evidence, especially one that closely matches a well-known pattern, is riskier than one an
   accurate perceiver withheld until more data arrived (P034); firsthand contact on an important
   issue can fix an image so firmly that later change goes undetected and means-to-an-end behavior
   gets misread as a permanent trait (P068, P138); and once a new image takes hold, past and
   present behavior gets reinterpreted to fit it, with what counts as obvious or as needing special
   explanation changing along with it (P166). Confirm the product is not simply asserting a hostile
   or benign reading without acknowledging that observers differ systematically in their
   propensity to see aggressiveness, and that a firmness-versus-conciliation dispute is often this
   difference in propensity rather than a values disagreement (P035). Before flagging any of this
   as unreasonable, however, confirm whether a reading that stretches ambiguous evidence to fit a
   popular hypothesis is in fact defensible, since a framework is what makes any pattern visible in
   a contradictory mass of evidence at all, and analysts who lack one are swayed by whatever trend
   is most recent (P168).
8. Check whether a fixed image is being used to discount signals that should update it, rather
   than being tested against them. Confirm the product has not dismissed an actor's explicit
   signals as having no predictive value merely because it is confident it already understands
   that actor — a policy disaster is often the clearest evidence an image was wrong all along, and
   by then it may be too late (P136); confirm it has not assumed others will discount an actor's
   stated intentions simply because they trust their own prediction of his behavior more (P125);
   and confirm that a hardening disagreement over what a single event means is named as such
   rather than left to harden further, since believing an inference rests on the event alone
   breeds intolerance of anyone who reads it differently (P029).
9. Check whether the product judges a message, warning, or action by how the sender meant it
   rather than by how the receiver's own beliefs about international relations and about the
   sender will actually shape its reading — a mis-estimate of the other's beliefs here produces
   confident wrongness about how one is seen (P032). Check the same asymmetry on the sending side:
   confirm a claim of successful influence is not resting mainly on the actor knowing his own
   effort far better than he knows the other's actual internal response, since absent strong
   contrary evidence that asymmetry alone makes "I was influential" the parsimonious-looking but
   unverified conclusion (P004).
10. Check that a reaction to a harmful act — or credit given for a helpful one — is graded by the
    intent the target actually infers, not by the act's raw size: mildest when the harm is read as
    unintended, harsher when the goal is read as merely self-interested, and most extreme when the
    harm is read as sought for its own sake (P092), since actors retaliate in proportion to the
    intent perceived almost regardless of the injury actually done, and the inferred motive — not
    the act itself — sets the scale of the response (P128); help, in the same way, only reads as a
    genuine positive signal when it was given freely and beyond what was required, not when it was
    compelled, accidental, or self-serving (P159). Confirm the product has not treated the basic
    reading of intent as the only variable — the details and context of the move, each side's
    operational code, and the specific goals attributed also shape how it lands, since even a
    conciliatory move can be read as weakness (P133).
11. When assembling the review, read the whole run of relevant reports as a series rather than
    judging each item in isolation, since a pattern invisible in any single message can emerge only
    across the sequence (P174).
12. Emit findings highest-impact first, each in the output-contract format below.

## Inputs

- The specific behavior, message, or event under review, and the actor(s) it concerns.
- The inference the product draws from it — a judgment of intent, disposition, coordination, or
  what a signal means.
- The image or standing view of the actor(s) already held by the analyst or product, including
  when and how it formed, if known.
- The immediate context bearing on the analyst at the time: the situation, a rehearsed plan, a
  cultural frame, or the emotional register of the moment, if reconstructable.
- Any prior reports or signals from the same run of events, so they can be read as a series rather
  than in isolation.
- What harm or help, if any, triggered the judgment, and what response or reaction is being
  calibrated to it.

## Output

Per finding: name the perceptual or attributional flaw (a centralized-actor or
alliance-coordination assumption, a self-fulfilling expectation, an evoked-set or affective
framing effect, an image that has hardened past its evidence or is overriding a live signal, a
misjudged sender/receiver asymmetry, a mis-scaled reaction to inferred intent, or an unreliable
self-report), apply the corrective technique named in the Procedure, state the residual
uncertainty the correction leaves, and end with a concrete next step. Order findings
highest-impact first. Never substitute this review for the substantive judgment about what the
actor will actually do.

## Anti-patterns to flag

- A review credits perception with causing an outcome that a substitutable trigger event explains
  just as well, treats an act as self-explanatory instead of asking what internal process produced
  it, or accepts "there was no choice" as proof the situation determined the response without
  checking whether anyone differently placed would have felt the same compulsion (P091, P127,
  P157, P108, P109).
- An official's stated reason for a decision, or his own forecast of what he will do next, is
  taken at face value instead of treated as an unreliable, possibly post-hoc account (P024, P130).
- A rival's unrelated moves, or its weapons budget, are read as executing one coherent plan, and
  the same excess coordination is projected onto an entire alliance or an agreement that actually
  rests on partners' differing images (P007, P013, P017, P081, P176, P175, P158).
- Ambiguous or minimal evidence is treated as confirming an adversary's expected move, a habitual
  pattern, or a belief that it won't fight, without checking whether the expectation itself — not
  the evidence — is doing the work (P005, P023, P164).
- The same behavior is read differently depending on unstated context — a preoccupation, a
  cultural template, a rehearsed plan, or the prevailing mood of a crisis — with no acknowledgment
  that this context, not the actor's disposition, is driving the reading (P096, P110, P170, P069).
- An early-formed image that happens to match a familiar pattern is treated as reliable, a firm
  image from past contact goes unquestioned as the actor's behavior or circumstances change, and a
  firmness-versus-conciliation dispute is treated as a values clash rather than a difference in
  propensity to see hostility — while a genuinely hypothesis-driven reading of ambiguous evidence
  is dismissed as bias when the framework behind it is what makes the pattern visible at all (P034,
  P068, P138, P166, P035, P168).
- New signals are discounted because the analyst already "understands" the actor, a stated
  intention is waved off because the observer trusts his own prediction more, or a disagreement
  over what one event means is left to harden into each side doubting the other's good faith
  (P136, P125, P029).
- A message is assessed by how the sender meant it, not by the receiver's actual beliefs, or a
  claim of successful influence rests only on the actor's own effort and ignores the far less
  visible response on the other side (P032, P004).
- A response to harm, or credit for help, is scaled to the act's size rather than the intent
  inferred from it, or a basic read of intent is treated as the whole story with no weight given to
  the operational code, context, or specific goals that also shape how a move lands (P092, P128,
  P159, P133).
- Individually weak or ambiguous reports are each judged in isolation, missing a pattern that only
  the series would reveal (P174).
