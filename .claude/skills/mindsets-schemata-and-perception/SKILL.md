# Mindsets Schemata And Perception

## Purpose

This skill reviews the mental models, schemata, and prior expectations through which the
analyst under review perceived the evidence — not the evidence's substance. It checks whether
an overwhelming or traumatic event produced an overgeneralized lesson that hardened into
standing doctrine (P002), whether a historical lesson or analogy was treated as determinative
of an outcome rather than as one probabilistic input among several (P016, P017), and whether a
policy, position, or filing scheme has outlived the premise that once justified it (P031,
P055). It also checks whether new information genuinely reassessed the analyst's own model
instead of being filtered through it unchanged, and whether the analyst's predictable
perceptual errors were actively guarded against (P045, P046, P025). This skill audits the
analyst's own frame, not how one actor reads or misreads another's signals or intentions, which
the sibling skill `perception-misperception-and-signaling` reviews.

## When to use

- A judgment leans on a vivid historical analogy, precedent, or "lesson of history" applied as
  though it will govern the outcome, rather than as one probabilistic input weighed against
  others (P016, P017).
- A judgment's doctrine or key lesson traces back to a traumatic, high-drive, or otherwise
  overwhelming event, or the write-up assesses a rare, high-impact scenario that has not been
  fully thought through (P002).
- An established policy, position, or way of categorizing information has persisted after the
  environment or premise that justified it changed, and it is unclear who should be the one to
  re-examine it (P031, P055).
- New information has arrived on an ongoing issue and the write-up shows the information being
  fitted into the existing model rather than the model's own premises being reassessed (P045,
  P046).
- The record gives no sign of the analyst's habits for catching their own predictable
  perceptual errors — examining supporting evidence, suspending judgment, consulting someone
  less invested in the outcome (P025).

## Procedure

1. Identify the schema under review: the doctrine, historical lesson, analogy, policy premise,
   or filing/categorization scheme the analyst used to make sense of the evidence. Fix this
   before judging whether it still fits the case.
2. If the judgment traces to a traumatic, high-drive, or otherwise overwhelming event, check
   whether the lesson drawn was overgeneralized or oversimplified because the event was too
   involving to yield a careful, principle-based lesson, and whether that lesson has since
   hardened into doctrine or language that has outlived the situation that produced it (P002).
3. Where the judgment invokes a historical lesson, confirm it was applied as one probabilistic
   input to the actor's likely behavior, not as a determinant of it — a lesson biases
   perception, it does not fix behavior, can be outweighed by other motives, and can fail to
   transfer even where it plainly applies (P016).
4. Where the judgment leans on an analogy or a "comparable case," confirm that in-depth
   analysis established the two situations as equivalent, rather than the analogy being
   accepted because it is vivid or because it papers over a gap in the evidence (P017).
5. Check whether an established policy, position, or subgoal is still being pursued after the
   environment changed and removed the premise that originally justified it, and whether its
   re-examination was assigned to someone not invested in the original effort rather than to
   those who built it (P031).
6. Check how the supporting information was labeled, filed, or categorized, and whether that
   scheme is itself hiding the information's true significance — ask whether a different
   categorization would surface it (P055).
7. Where new information has arrived on an ongoing issue, confirm the analyst reassessed the
   premises of the model itself rather than filtering the new information through it unchanged,
   and — where a long-stable model now faces a changed environment — confirm the old schema was
   actually unlearned rather than merely patched, since unlearning is harder than the original
   learning was (P045, P046).
8. Confirm the write-up or process shows the basic safeguards against the analyst's own
   predictable perceptual errors: the supporting evidence for a quickly formed image was
   examined, judgment was suspended rather than rushed, someone less invested in the outcome
   was consulted, and evidence merely consistent with the favored image was not treated as
   having disconfirmed the alternatives (P025).
9. Emit findings highest-impact first, each naming the flaw, the failure it enables, the
   principle it rests on, the corrective, and the residual uncertainty, closing with a concrete
   next step.

## Inputs

- The analytic judgment or forecast under review, together with the schema it visibly or
  implicitly rests on: a doctrine, a historical lesson, an analogy, a policy premise, or a
  categorization scheme.
- The traumatic, high-drive, or high-impact event (if any) behind that doctrine or lesson, and
  roughly when the lesson was formed relative to when it is being applied now.
- The precedent or comparable case being drawn on, and any analysis on record showing the two
  situations were checked for equivalence rather than assumed.
- The history of the policy, position, or subgoal under review: when and why it was adopted,
  whether the environment has since changed, and who is doing the re-examining.
- How the underlying information was categorized or filed, and whether an alternative
  categorization was ever considered.
- Whether new information has arrived on the issue since the model was formed, and any evidence
  that the analyst reassessed the model itself rather than just fitting the new information
  into it.

## Output

Per finding: name the mind-set flaw (an overgeneralized lesson minted from a high-drive event,
a historical lesson or analogy treated as determinative rather than probabilistic, a policy or
filing scheme that has outlived its premise, or a model left unreassessed against new
information), apply the correction (test the lesson's applicability as probabilistic and
re-examine who owns that test, confirm analogical equivalence through in-depth analysis,
reassign re-examination to someone not invested in the original effort, reassess the model's
premises directly, or refile the information under an alternative category), state the residual
uncertainty the correction leaves, and end with a concrete next step. Order findings
highest-impact first. Never close a review with the substantive judgment in place of this
structure.

## Anti-patterns to flag

- A doctrine or "lesson" minted from a single overwhelming, high-drive event that has gone
  unquestioned ever since — the event stamped the organization once, and the lesson has
  outlived the situation that produced it (P002).
- A historical lesson invoked as though it determines an actor's future behavior, with no
  allowance that a lesson only biases perception, can be outweighed by other motives, or can
  fail to transfer even where it plainly applies (P016).
- A vivid precedent or analogy accepted as equivalent to the present case without the in-depth
  analysis needed to confirm the two situations are actually comparable (P017).
- No visible safeguard against the analyst's own predictable perceptual errors: supporting
  evidence for a quick image never examined, judgment never suspended, no less-invested
  colleague consulted, and evidence merely consistent with the favored view treated as
  disconfirming the alternatives (P025).
- A policy, position, or subgoal still pursued after the environment that justified it has
  changed, re-examined (if at all) only by the people invested in building it rather than by
  someone positioned to see it is no longer needed (P031).
- New information on an ongoing issue absorbed by filtering it through the existing model
  instead of reassessing the model's premises, or a long-stable schema kept in place against a
  changed environment because unlearning it is harder than the original learning was (P045,
  P046).
- Information's true significance hidden by the category or file it was placed under, with no
  check of whether a different categorization would have surfaced it (P055).
